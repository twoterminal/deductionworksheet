How it works

From four core inputs to the IRS worksheet result

The IRS Publication 560 worksheet starts with net profit and the deduction for self-employment tax, then calculates net earnings, applies a reduced self-employed contribution rate, and applies the applicable limits. This calculator performs those intermediate steps for you.

Why the plan contribution rate matters

The IRS does not simply multiply your net profit by the plan's stated contribution rate. For a self-employed individual, the IRS uses a reduced contribution rate because the contribution itself affects the compensation calculation.

Self-employed rate plan rate ÷ (1 + plan rate)

For example, an 8.5% plan contribution rate becomes 0.085. The worksheet then adds 1 to get 1.085 and divides 0.085 by 1.085. The result is rounded to at least three decimal places before being used in the deduction worksheet.

FAQ

Self-Employed Deduction Calculator FAQs

What is a Self-Employed Deduction Calculator?

It is an independent calculator that follows the IRS Deduction Worksheet for Self-Employed methodology to estimate the maximum deductible contribution for an eligible self-employed individual.

How many numbers do I need to enter?

For the fastest route, the calculator asks for four core inputs: tax year, net business profit, plan contribution rate, and the deduction for one-half of self-employment tax from Schedule 1, line 15. Additional fields appear only when elective deferrals or catch-up contributions apply.

Why do I need the deduction for one-half of self-employment tax?

The IRS worksheet explicitly uses the deduction for self-employment tax from Schedule 1, Form 1040, line 15 as Step 2. Using that amount avoids making assumptions about the taxpayer's complete Schedule SE situation.

Does the calculator calculate the self-employed rate automatically?

Yes. For whole-number contribution rates, the calculator uses the IRS Rate Table. For a rate such as 8.5%, it uses the Rate Worksheet formula and rounds the resulting self-employed rate to at least three decimal places before applying it to the deduction worksheet.

Can I use this for a SEP IRA?

The IRS Publication 560 worksheet states that a SEP plan is treated as a profit-sharing plan for this worksheet. The IRS also cautions that the worksheet applies only to self-employed individuals with one defined contribution plan and should not be used for SARSEPs.

Is this an IRS calculator?

No. This is an independent educational tool based on IRS publications and worksheets. It is not operated, endorsed, or approved by the IRS.

Why might my result differ from tax software?

Tax software may have additional information about your tax return, other businesses, wages, plan participation, or special circumstances. This calculator is intentionally focused on the Publication 560 self-employed deduction worksheet and should be verified against your current tax forms and instructions.
